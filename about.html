The Architecture of Operations

A non-traditional career path built on analyzing digital logic, mastering physical production, and navigating federal procurement.

CAGE: 180J1

Megan French

Operations & Systems Specialist

I am a systems thinker who refuses to be confined to a desk. I believe you cannot optimize a supply chain from a spreadsheet unless you understand the friction of the factory floor. My objective is to bridge the gap between high-level digital strategy and raw, industrial execution to modernize complex logistical projects.

01. The Digital Era

For over six years, I operated exclusively in the digital realm as a Web Content Producer for a major technology agency.

I managed the production, automation, and monetization strategies for live web platforms. This environment taught me how to scale systems to meet demand, optimize digital assets globally, and design workflows that dictate human action through UI/UX principles. I learned exactly how data moves.

02. The Factory Floor

Data is only half the equation. To truly grasp the backbone of the American supply chain, I stepped out of the server room and into heavy industry.

As a press operator in a regional manufacturing hub, I run high-tonnage injection molding presses. It is an unforgiving environment requiring meticulous quality control, secondary processing, and rigorous downtime tracking. It is hands-on research in physical asset management and mechanical friction. I learned how things actually move.

03. The Synthesis

Bridging digital logic with physical manufacturing leads directly to the ultimate logistical puzzle: Federal Procurement.

By securing a CAGE code and studying the Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR), I am synthesizing my two backgrounds. My goal is to leverage tech-based OSINT, LPTA (Lowest Price Technically Acceptable) cost logic, and an intimate understanding of the factory floor to streamline complex federal supply chains.
